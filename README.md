## The fine print that costs people their cash back

This is where the real money gets lost, so read this part before your first order:

- **Don't use Safari.** TopCashback's own retailer pages flag Safari as unreliable for tracking and recommend Chrome instead.
- **Skip third-party coupon sites.** Applying promo codes from other coupon websites can break the tracking chain and void the cash back entirely. Retailer-owned codes applied at the retailer's own checkout are the safe exception.
- **Turn off ad blockers** for the shopping session, and don't wander to other sites mid-checkout. Both are common causes of untracked orders.
- **Keep your order confirmation screenshots.** Individual transactions do sometimes fail to track, and a screenshot is what supports a missing-cash-back claim.
- **Cash back isn't guaranteed until the retailer confirms it.** If you return the laptop, the cash back disappears too. Buy the config you intend to keep.
- **Check the category, not just the store.** Walmart's 2% electronics rate is real but modest; its 8% rate applies to Flash Deals categories, which rotate. On a $2,000 gaming laptop, that difference is $40 versus $160.

One honest limitation: Amazon, the biggest deal destination of the season, frequently shows little or no cash back through any major portal. If your target is a Walmart- or Best Buy-sold laptop (which covers Lenovo, HP, Acer, ASUS, and most MacBook listings), the cash-back layer works well. If it's an Amazon-exclusive flash deal, take the discount and don't force it.
